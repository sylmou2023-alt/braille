The Braille Alphabet

Braille is read by touch. Each character is a cell of six possible raised dots, arranged in two columns of three. The dots are always numbered the same way, and each letter is described by the numbers of its raised dots.

123456

Left column 1–2–3, right column 4–5–6, top to bottom.

Letters

A to Z

The alphabet is built in three “lines” of ten. Learn the first ten letters and the rest follow a pattern.

Line 1 · a–juses only the top four dots (1, 2, 4, 5)
Line 2 · k–t= a–j + dot 3
Line 3 · u–z= a–e + dots 3 and 6, except w

Why is w the odd one out? Louis Braille invented the system in France in the 1820s, and French did not use the letter w at the time. It was added later, outside the pattern.

Numbers

Digits 1 to 0

Braille has no separate digits. Put the number sign (dots 3-4-5-6) in front, and the letters a–j then mean 1–0.

Signs

Capitals and punctuation

A capital letter is shown by putting the capital sign (dot 6) before it. Two capital signs make the whole word uppercase.

Grade 2 · contracted braille

Short forms for common words

Most English books are printed in contracted braille, which saves space. When a letter stands alone as a word, it means a whole word. A few cells have their own word meanings too.

Try it

Write your own text in braille

Uses uncontracted (grade 1) braille. Accents are removed, and ⇧ marks a capital sign and # marks a number sign.

French braille uses the same 26 letters. The difference is in the extra signs: in French braille, the cells for English “and”, “for”, “of”, “the” and “with” are accented letters (ç, é, à, è, ù).
